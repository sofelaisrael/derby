Privacy Policy

This policy explains what information the Derby Bins app ("the app") collects, how it is used, and the choices you have. It applies to the mobile app on iOS and Android.

Last updated: 8 October 2026

1. Who is responsible

The app is provided by Augustine Igharo, trading as Derby Bins. You can contact Derby Bins at contactxomo@gmail.com.

Augustine Igharo is the data controller for the app: the person who decides what information is collected, what it is used for, and how long it is kept.

2. What the app does

The app shows bin collection schedules for nine councils across Derbyshire and Derby City — Derby City Council, Erewash Borough Council, Amber Valley Borough Council, High Peak Borough Council, Derbyshire Dales District Council, Bolsover District Council, Chesterfield Borough Council, South Derbyshire District Council and North East Derbyshire District Council. It also shows a short weather forecast, can send optional reminder notifications, and includes a form you can use to report a problem such as a bin that was not collected.

3. Information Derby Bins collects, and why

The app cannot work entirely on your device, because showing you your collection dates means asking the council that collects your bins. The list below is everything the app sends to us or to a third party, plus the one web address it can hand to your browser instead of to us. Nothing else leaves your device.

  • Postcode and address, to look up your schedule. When you enter your postcode and pick your address, the app sends your postcode and that address's public property identifier (a UPRN) to our server, which passes them on to the council's own online lookup. Your actual collection dates cannot be obtained any other way.
  • Which area you are in, for two of the nine councils. Bolsover District Council and North East Derbyshire District Council publish no address-based lookup. Both publish fixed collection calendar areas covering named lists of towns, so for those two the app asks which area covers you instead of sending a postcode to be looked up.
  • One fixed coordinate, for the weather. The app sends a latitude and longitude to Open-Meteo. Those coordinates come from a small table of council-area centre points built into the app, and they are not your location: the app requests no GPS or location permission on Android or iOS, and the coordinate does not change when you move.
  • What you type into the Report an issue form. If you use Report an issue, you provide your name (required), your email address (required), the type of issue (required) and a description (optional). That form offers six issue types: Missing bin, Bin not collected, Bin damaged, Wrong bin collected, Collection schedule wrong, Other. Your address, council name and postcode are attached automatically from your profile rather than typed by you. The report is sent to our server and written to a private Google spreadsheet that we control, so that we can check the problem against the council's published information and reply to you. Reports are never passed to councils.
  • What you type into the Report missing address form. When an address lookup returns nothing, the app offers you a second form, Report missing address. It asks for your name (required) and your email address (required) again, plus the address itself — prefilled from the address you were trying to find, and editable, because correcting it is the reason the form exists — and any notes (optional). Its issue type is fixed to Missing address, which is a seventh type that the Report an issue form does not offer. This form reaches the same private Google spreadsheet as the other one, and its reports are never passed to councils either.
  • Your postcode, for North East Derbyshire District Council only. That council's area is split into North and South collection calendars, so when the app has a postcode but no calendar selection it sends that postcode to Ordnance Survey Places to identify which of the two areas applies. This is one council out of nine: the other eight councils' address searches run entirely on the council's own website and never reach Ordnance Survey. A postcode is sent to a public address gazetteer, not to a location lookup.
  • Your postcode, in a web address, if you ask for it. If an address lookup returns nothing, the app can offer to open uprn.uk, a public UPRN lookup site, in your browser with your postcode in the link. Nothing reaches us when you do: the app hands the web address to your device's browser and stops there. That page is outside our control, its own privacy policy applies to it, and we are not responsible for what it does with the request. Check the address on screen before you follow the link.

4. Information stored on your device

Everything in this section is stored only on your device, using local app storage. None of it is sent to us:

  • Your postcode.
  • The address you selected and its UPRN.
  • Your council name.
  • Your reminder settings, and which reminders have already been shown.
  • Your light or dark preference.
  • Whether you have finished onboarding.
  • A cached list of the councils the app supports, refreshed at most once a day.
  • A cached copy of your most recent collection schedule, so the app can open without a connection. The app stops using this copy once it is more than seven days old. Separately, the raw server reply to that same lookup is kept for up to 24 hours, purely so the app can avoid asking the server again inside that window — a second, shorter-timer copy of the same schedule, on your device and not on our server.

Reminders are scheduled and shown by your device; they are not sent through us. The app can add a collection to your device's calendar, and can share a schedule using your device's share sheet, but only when you ask it to. To remove any of the information in this section, clear the app's storage in your device settings or delete the app.

5. What Derby Bins does not collect

  • No advertising, and no advertising or device identifier.
  • No analytics, telemetry, usage measurement or crash reporting.
  • No account, no sign-in and no profile.
  • No GPS and no precise location. The app requests no location permission on Android or iOS.
  • No contacts, photos, camera, microphone or files.
  • No background monitoring.
  • No cookies and no tracking scripts on this website.

This is not aspirational. The app contains no analytics SDK, no crash-reporting SDK, no advertising SDK and no social login, and the website sets no cookies because it loads no script capable of setting one.

6. Service providers (processors)

To make the app work, limited information passes through the following service providers. That list is every third party we know the app passes anything to, not a standing guarantee that nothing new will ever appear: where an address lookup fails, the app can hand a web address to your own browser and to a site we do not run, and that site is named here precisely because it is outside our control.

  • Render — hosts the small server that relays council lookups and receives report submissions.
  • The nine councils listed in section 2 — receive your postcode and address identifier, and only so they can return your collection schedule. Each council's own page is linked on our official information sources page.
  • Open-Meteo — receives the fixed council-area coordinate described in section 3.
  • Google Apps Script and Google Sheets — receive report submissions and store them in a private spreadsheet.
  • Ordnance Survey Places — receives a postcode, and only for North East Derbyshire District Council, to identify which collection calendar area applies.
  • uprn.uk — a public UPRN lookup site the app can open in your browser with your postcode in the link, when an address lookup returns nothing. It is not a service we operate: we have no contract with it, no control over it, and no way to see what it does with the request beyond your postcode appearing in the web address. Its own privacy policy governs it, not this one.

Derby Bins does not sell, rent or trade personal information, and no provider is permitted to use the information for its own purposes. What a given council does with the postcode it receives is covered by that council's own privacy notice, not by this one.

7. How long information is kept

On our server. Schedule lookups are not stored: they are not written down and they are not cached. Address lookups are not stored either. They are passed to the council and returned to the app, and nothing is kept — not on disk, and not in the server's memory. There is no account, no identifier and no record of what you looked up.

Report submissions. A report you send is written to the private Google spreadsheet described in section 6. How long we keep it has not been decided yet, and this policy does not state a period it cannot yet honour. Ask us and we will tell you what is currently held.

On your device. Everything listed in section 4 stays on your device until you clear the app's storage or delete the app. The two time-limited items are both copies of your collection schedule: the app stops using the seven-day copy once it is more than seven days old, and the raw 24-hour copy once it is more than 24 hours old.

8. Children

Derby Bins is not directed at children and is not intended for anyone under 13. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children under 13. If a child has sent us personal information through the report form, email contactxomo@gmail.com and we will delete it.

9. Your rights

Under the UK GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018 you have the right to:

  • ask for access to the personal information we hold about you;
  • ask us to correct personal information that is inaccurate;
  • ask us to erase personal information we hold about you;
  • ask us to restrict how we use your personal information;
  • object to our use of your personal information;
  • receive your personal information in a portable format.

Because most of what the app knows is stored on your device and never reaches us, the quickest way to see or remove it is to clear the app's storage or delete the app. For anything we do hold — for example a report you have submitted — email contactxomo@gmail.com.

You also have the right to complain to the Information Commissioner's Office (ico.org.uk (opens in a new tab)).

10. Changes to this policy

Derby Bins may update this policy. The "Last updated" date at the top of this page changes whenever it does. The version that applies to you is the one published here at the time you used the app.

Questions about this policy? Email contactxomo@gmail.com.
